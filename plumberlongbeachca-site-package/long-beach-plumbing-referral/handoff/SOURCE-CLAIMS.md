# Source claims

Checked November 8, 2026. These are the factual claims the public pages rely on, the source, and the limit. Pages do not claim field experience, a crew, a price, or current referral acceptance.

## Long Beach Utilities — sewer

- Source: https://www.lbutilities.org/sewer
- The utility says it owns and operates the sanitary sewer, cites more than 700 miles of sewer lines, and describes wastewater delivered to the county sanitation districts.
- Lateral split used on the site: building to sidewalk/property line, commonly about 4 inches, on private property, is the owner’s responsibility. Property line to the center of the street or alley, commonly about 6 inches, on city property, is shared. The owner is responsible for routine maintenance of both sections. The city maintains the main and describes work on laterals on city property.
- Emergency number published on that page: (562) 570-2390 for sewer emergencies and overflows, noisy manholes, neighborhood sewer odor, and insects from a manhole.
- Fats, oils, and grease can clog the main, and a property owner whose discharge damages the main can be held responsible.
- Limit: “commonly” is the utility’s word for diameter, not a measurement of any address. The policy can change. This is not a claim determination. It is not the rule for Signal Hill, Lakewood, Seal Beach, Los Alamitos, or Carson.

## Long Beach Utilities — customer service and contact

- Sources: https://www.lbutilities.org/customer-service and https://www.lbutilities.org/contact-us
- Water and sewer emergency (562) 570-2390. The customer-service page describes that line as available at all hours and lists sewer stoppage on the property and sewage in the street among the examples.
- Gas emergency (562) 570-2140. The contact page says to leave the area and call from a safe place, or call 911, and not to use switches, flames, or a phone in the odor.
- Gas customer line (562) 570-2100 is the number the drinking-water page gives gas customers for hot-water trouble when there is no odor, with the after-hours gas emergency number beside it.
- Call 811 before digging.
- Limit: these are Long Beach Utilities numbers. Nearby cities are told not to assume the same utility. The utility’s all-hours statement is not a statement about this referral service.

## Long Beach Utilities — drinking water

- Source: https://www.lbutilities.org/water/water-quality/your-drinking-water
- The utility calls the drinking water “moderately hard” to “hard,” meaning dissolved minerals such as calcium and magnesium. No hardness number was copied because the page did not give one.
- The utility delivers unheated water. It points gas customers to (562) 570-2100 and after hours (562) 570-2140, and electric customers to Southern California Edison at (800) 655-4555.
- It says it is responsible up to the meter, that it does not use lead service lines in its distribution system, and that lead can still come from home plumbing and fixtures.
- Filter owners are told to maintain equipment. Salt softeners add sodium; the utility says people with specified health conditions should ask a doctor. That caution is quoted as the utility’s, not as medical advice from this site.
- Limit: not a water test, not a safety warning, and not a description of another city’s supply.

## Sewer connection permits

- Source: https://www.lbutilities.org/Development-Services/Sewer-Development-Services/install-sewer-connection
- New connections involve a utility review, drawings, possible capacity review, fees, and an excavation permit through Long Beach Public Works. The page describes multi-week processing, not a same-day permit.
- Limit: the site tells readers to use that process. It does not restate the fee schedule or say a particular repair is exempt.

## Building permits and historic districts

- Permit center: https://longbeach.gov/lbcd/building/permit-center/ (the city’s permitcenter URL redirects here). Building-permits phone (562) 570-5237 appears on the city’s published contact lists and on the utility’s sewer-connection page.
- Historic districts: https://www.longbeach.gov/lbcd/planning/preservation/districts/ and preservation phone (562) 570-6194.
- Facts used, all from that districts page: Drake Park/Willmore and the 1881 plan; Belmont Heights dates and streets; Bluff Park (1982, Ocean Boulevard and 2nd, Junipero to Loma); Bluff Heights streets and the Alamitos Beach Townsite note; California Heights size, 1920s boom, Wardlow, Bixby, Lime, Gardenia, and houses moved from Signal Hill; Rose Park and Rose Park South dates and streets; Grant Neighborhood as the 6000 block of Walnut, designated in 2019, first North Long Beach landmark district; Wrigley cottages, 1928–1934, Eucalyptus and the two numbered addresses.
- Limit: district boundaries are preservation boundaries. They are not pipe inventories. Interior work is not automatically reviewed or automatically exempt.
