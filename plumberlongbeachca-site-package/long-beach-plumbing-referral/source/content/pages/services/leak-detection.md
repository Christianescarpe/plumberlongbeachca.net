A drip under one sink and a stain that grows while every faucet is off are different leaks. This guide helps you describe which one you have, what an inspection can look at, and when Long Beach Utilities should hear about water that is still on the utility side of the meter.

## Leaks Detected & Repaired {#leaks-detected-repaired}

Start with what you can see without opening a wall. Is the water only under one sink, only at the toilet supply, or also in a ceiling below a bathroom? Does it appear when a fixture is used, or when every fixture is off? A supply stop that drips when the fixture is closed is a fixture-side issue. A stain that grows while the house is vacant is a different question, and it still does not prove a slab leak.

Do not treat this page as a locator. Listening devices, moisture meters, pressure tests, and visual inspection are tools a provider may use. None of them is guaranteed, and none of them is promised as non-invasive. Some leaks are found by opening the surface. If a quote says the search will not open anything, ask what happens if the search fails. That sentence belongs in the estimate.

Repair comes after a location the provider is willing to stand behind. A detected drip at a shutoff is a small repair. A leak in a buried private line is the [underground pipe](/services/pipe-water-line-repair/#underground-pipe-repairs) conversation, including locate and permit questions. A leak in the sewer lateral is not a supply leak at all. Send that one to the [sewer guide](/services/sewer-repair/#sewer-lines).

## Plumbing Inspection {#plumbing-inspection}

A plumbing inspection is a defined look at fixtures, visible piping, water heater condition, and the symptoms you describe. It is not a sewer camera, and it is not a certification that every hidden pipe is sound. Ask what will be opened, what will only be observed, and what the written notes will say. “Whole-house inspection” without a list is too vague to compare.

The [fixture guide](/services/fixtures-remodel-plumbing/#general-plumbing-repair) is the right page when the inspection is really a dripping valve, a running toilet, or a disposal. The [water heater guide](/services/water-heaters/#water-heater-repair-or-service) is the right page when the symptom is rust-colored hot water or a tank that will not stay lit. Mixing those into one “leak” label makes the quote muddy.

Authorization matters in attached housing. A ceiling stain in a [downtown](/areas/downtown-alamitos-beach/) condo may start in the unit above. The person who can let a provider in may be a neighbor, a manager, or an association. Bring that constraint to the call. This site will not contact a building for you.

## When the utility, not a contractor, should hear about it {#when-the-utility-should-hear}

Long Beach Utilities says it is responsible for drinking water up to the meter, and that it does not use lead service lines in its distribution system. Lead can still come from plumbing and fixtures inside a building. That is a reason to ask what the house piping is. It is not a finding that your water is unsafe, and this site does not test water.

Water in the street, a broken hydrant, or no water to the property for a reason other than nonpayment is on the utility’s emergency list at [(562) 570-2390](tel:+15625702390). A spinning meter while every faucet is closed can mean a private leak past the meter, which is a contractor question, or a meter issue, which is a utility question. Describe it. Do not decide the boundary from the sidewalk.

Discolored water after nearby main work is something the utility tells customers to flush. If only one faucet is discolored, the utility says the cause is more likely inside the building. That distinction is more useful than a generic “we find leaks” claim.

:::faq
Q: There is no puddle. Can there still be a leak?
A: Yes. A meter that moves with the fixtures off, a stain that grows, or the sound of water in a wall can be a leak you cannot see. Those are reasons to ask for an inspection. They are not a map of the pipe. The provider has to test and tell you what was ruled out. This page cannot do that remotely.

Q: Is detection the same visit as the repair?
A: Not always. Finding a likely location is one scope. Opening a wall, a yard, or a slab is another, and it may need a separate approval once the location is known. Ask the quote to split search time from repair time. A single price that assumes the answer is how disputes start.

Q: What does an inspection actually include?
A: Ask for the list: which fixtures, whether the meter will be watched, whether any panel or access door will be opened, and whether a sewer camera is included. A camera is a different service, described on the sewer page. An honest inspection also says what it did not see. Hidden pipe is still hidden if nobody opened it.

Q: What drives the cost of a leak visit?
A: How hard the water is to reach, how long the search takes, whether a surface has to be opened, and whether the repair is a valve or a buried line. Permits and restoration matter if the work leaves the fixture. No Long Beach flat rate is published here because the provider has not authorized one.

Q: When should I stop and call the utility or leave the house?
A: Call Long Beach Utilities for water in the street, a suspected main break, or no water that is not a shutoff for nonpayment. If you smell gas near a water heater or piping, leave and use the gas emergency number from a safe place. A referral conversation is not the right channel for either of those.
:::
