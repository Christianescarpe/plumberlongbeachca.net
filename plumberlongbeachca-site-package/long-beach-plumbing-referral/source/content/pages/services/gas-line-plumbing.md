If you smell gas, leave and call Long Beach Utilities or 911. That is not a sales call. Planned piping for a stove, a dryer, or a heater, with no odor, is the plumbing conversation on this page: route, access, permit, and whether a provider is qualified to do that work. This page does not include a leak test.

Long Beach Utilities publishes the local gas emergency number, [(562) 570-2140](tel:+15625702140). Its contact guidance says to take other people with you, leave the area, and call from a safe place. Do not use switches, flames, or a phone where the odor is. Do not look for the leak with a lighter, a match, or a homemade bubble test. Those are not steps this site will dress up as advice.

If you are unsure whether the odor is gas, treat it as gas until the utility or the fire department says otherwise. Do not stay in the building to arrange a plumbing visit.

## Gas Lines Installed & Repaired {#gas-lines-installed-repaired}

:::conditional
Planned gas piping is described so it is not confused with an emergency. Whether a provider installs or repairs gas lines, and under what license, has to be confirmed with that provider.
:::

Planned work means a known appliance project with no active leak: a stove, a dryer, a heater, or a similar connection where the piping has to be extended, altered, or repaired. The provider has to see the meter, the route, the appliance rating, and the shutoff. This website does not size pipe and does not approve a route through a wall.

A quote for planned work should name the appliance, the approximate route, whether the meter or the regulator has to change, and who pulls the permit and meets the inspector. Long Beach building permits are handled through the [permit center](https://longbeach.gov/lbcd/building/permit-center/), [(562) 570-5237](tel:+15625705237). Gas utility questions stay with Long Beach Utilities. A contractor and the utility are not substitutes for each other.

Repair of existing appliance piping, when there is no odor and the utility has not told you to leave, is still a qualified-trades question. Ask the provider what license covers the work and whether an inspection is required. Contractor advertising rules are the [CSLB’s](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/resources/guidesandpublications/advertisingguidelines.pdf), and this referral service is not displaying a contractor license of its own. Do not read this page as a claim that a particular license is on the call.

Water-heater gas connectors are a frequent reason people ask about piping. The heater decision itself is on the [water heater page](/services/water-heaters/#water-heater-install-or-replace). If the heater will not light and you do not smell gas, the utility’s published gas customer number is [(562) 570-2100](tel:+15625702100). Smell gas, and you are back to the emergency number and 911.

Access is part of the quote. A meter in a side yard, a line in a ceiling, or a condo shaft that a manager must open will not take the same time as an exposed garage run. [Downtown](/areas/downtown-alamitos-beach/) and [Belmont Shore](/areas/belmont-shore-heights/) buildings often add a manager or an association. Say that before anyone schedules. Neighboring cities may not be served by Long Beach’s municipal gas utility. [Signal Hill](/areas/signal-hill/), [Lakewood](/areas/lakewood/), [Seal Beach](/areas/seal-beach/), [Los Alamitos](/areas/los-alamitos/), and [Carson](/areas/carson/) each need their own utility confirmed. Do not hand them this city’s gas number as if it were universal.

:::context
Heading: Long Beach context for gas piping
Long Beach Utilities is the published gas utility for Long Beach and operates the emergency line cited above. That is a municipal fact. It is not a statement that every address near the city limit is on the same system. Signal Hill is its own city. Confirm the utility from the address.

This page does not use water hardness, housing age, or soil conditions to sell gas work. Those facts belong on heater, fixture, and sewer guides when they are relevant. Gas piping is a fuel-safety and permit question. Historic-district rules can matter if a new vent or meter location changes an exterior. Ask preservation at [(562) 570-6194](tel:+15625706194) about that visible scope. They do not apply to the act of leaving a house that smells of gas.

:::faq
Q: I smell gas. Should I use this referral service?
A: No. Get people out, avoid switches and flames, and do not use a phone in the odor. From a safe place call Long Beach Utilities at (562) 570-2140 or call 911. Do not look for the leak yourself. This preview’s referral control does not dial, and it is the wrong tool even after a number exists.

Q: What does a planned gas-line visit need to know?
A: Which appliance, whether there is any odor, who can approve the work, and how the meter and the route can be reached. Photos of the appliance rating plate can help a provider prepare. They do not replace a look at the piping. If there is an odor, skip the photos and leave. Planned work is only the no-leak case.

Q: Who permits and inspects a new appliance line?
A: In Long Beach, building-permit questions go to the permit center, and the gas utility remains Long Beach Utilities. The contractor should say which permit applies and who meets the inspector. This page cannot clear an address. Work in another city uses that city’s counter and that city’s gas utility, which you should confirm rather than assume.

Q: What changes the quote for planned piping?
A: Route length, whether the line is exposed, meter or regulator changes, wall or ceiling access, permits, and the appliance’s demand. A condo or a historic exterior can add an approval before work starts. Get those items on the written quote. No price and no “same day gas crew” claim belongs on this site.
:::
