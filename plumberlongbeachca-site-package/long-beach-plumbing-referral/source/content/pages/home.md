## What to sort out before you call {#what-to-sort-out}

This is the page for general plumbing help in Long Beach. A single clog, a sewer lateral, a water heater, or a faucet each has its own guide, so you can read the decision that matches the symptom instead of starting from a blank description.

If you smell gas, leave and call Long Beach Utilities or 911 from a safe place. If sewage is in the street or a manhole is overflowing, call the utility water and sewer line. Those are city emergency numbers, printed in the safety note. A plumbing referral is for private property work a contractor can quote.

## Where the guides actually go {#where-the-guides-go}

Six Long Beach guides are editorial groupings, not official boundaries. Together they discuss eleven residential ZIP sections: 90802, 90803, 90804, 90805, 90806, 90807, 90808, 90810, 90813, 90814, and 90815. A ZIP is a postal area. It does not prove a house is inside the city, and it does not prove a provider accepts the address.

[Downtown and Alamitos Beach](/areas/downtown-alamitos-beach/) contrasts Willmore and Drake Park's early housing with apartment and condo access. [Belmont Shore and the Heights](/areas/belmont-shore-heights/) keeps the coastal grid separate from the City's historic-district records for Belmont Heights, Bluff Park, and Bluff Heights. [Bixby Knolls and California Heights](/areas/bixby-knolls-california-heights/) uses the City's account of 1920s houses and the mature streetscape. [East Long Beach](/areas/east-long-beach/) does not treat Rose Park, Los Altos, and El Dorado as the same era. [North Long Beach](/areas/north-long-beach/) keeps the small Grant historic block from standing in for all of 90805. [Wrigley and west-central Long Beach](/areas/wrigley-west-central/) separates a two-block cottage district from other central and west property types.

[Signal Hill](/areas/signal-hill/), [Lakewood](/areas/lakewood/), [Seal Beach](/areas/seal-beach/), [Los Alamitos](/areas/los-alamitos/), and [Carson](/areas/carson/) are separate municipalities. Long Beach permit language and the utility's lateral policy are not their rules. Whether any of those places is accepted on a live call is unverified.

:::faq
Q: Are you the plumber who would do the work?
A: No. This site is an independent referral service, not a plumbing contractor and not a city department. If a call is connected, a separate provider decides whether to accept it, what the price is, and what the license and scope cover. You would contract with and pay that provider directly.

Q: How do I ask whether someone can take the job?
A: Use the phone button on this page. There is no form. Have the address, who can approve the visit, and a short description of the drain, leak, or heater. The provider sets the price and the schedule. Utility emergency numbers in the safety note are for gas and for sewer or water emergencies.

Q: Can you tell me what the job will cost?
A: No. A useful estimate depends on access, what the fixture or pipe actually is, whether a camera or permit is involved, and who may approve the work. Those facts belong in the provider's quote. This site does not publish a price list or a typical Long Beach total.

Q: Do you already know my ZIP is accepted?
A: The guides name eleven Long Beach residential ZIP sections and five nearby cities so the site has a map you can read. That list is not a live coverage feed. Acceptance for houses, condos, rentals, and businesses has to be confirmed when a real line is answered.

Q: What should I do before I describe a backup or a gas smell?
A: If you smell gas, leave the area and call Long Beach Utilities or 911 from a safe place. If sewage is in the street or a manhole is overflowing, call the utility water and sewer emergency line. For a private fixture, note who can approve entry before you ask a contractor to visit.
