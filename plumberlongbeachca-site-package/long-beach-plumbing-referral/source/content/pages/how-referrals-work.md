A plumbing referral, as this site means it, is an introduction by phone. You describe the property and the problem. If a provider accepts that kind of call, you talk to the provider. The provider decides whether to come, what it will cost, and what the contract says. You would sign and pay that provider. This website is not the contractor and does not undertake the construction itself or through a crew of its own.

That limit is deliberate. The Contractors State License Board has published guidance for online referral marketplaces: they are not to offer to undertake construction themselves or through others, and they are not to solicit or negotiate the construction contract on the contractor’s behalf. Customers should contract with and pay the licensed contractor directly. Contractor advertisements have their own license-number rules. This page is not a legal opinion and not a safe harbor. Counsel has not signed off on a live script, a domain, or a routing pattern, because none of those is final. Older CSLB notes also use dollar thresholds this site does not rely on.

The public PDFs are the [online marketplace fast facts](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/resources/industrybulletins/online_marketplace_fast_facts.pdf) and the [advertising guidelines](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/resources/guidesandpublications/advertisingguidelines.pdf). Read them as the board’s documents. The operating rule is stricter than a slogan: no price negotiation, no promise that someone will arrive at a named hour, and no license number displayed for a company that is not the contractor.

## What the caller should hear {#what-the-caller-should-hear}

On a live line, the opening should make the referral role obvious before any connection. The address, the city, the symptom, and the caller’s authority are the useful facts. A gas odor or sewage in the street should be redirected to the utility or 911, not kept on the line to manufacture a billable conversation. The [contact page](/contact/) lists the Long Beach numbers and says when they do not apply.

If no provider accepts the ZIP, the building type, or the specialty, the call should end without a fake appointment. Conditional topics — wells, hydronic radiant, backflow testing, sump pumps, manufactured homes, pool shell work, jetting, trenchless methods — are explanations on the service pages, not items a script may promise. The [specialty guide](/services/specialty-plumbing/#well-pumps) is where those limits are written.

## Compensation, without a public rate {#compensation}

This kind of service may be paid by a network or by a provider when a call meets that payer’s rules. The caller is not asked to pay this website a fee on top of the contractor’s invoice. The contractor’s price is the contractor’s price.

The amount, the timing, what counts as a qualified call, and what is refunded are not verified for publication. They are not printed here, and they will not be printed as a teaser. Publishing an unverified rate would mislead both callers and the people operating the line. Operations has to confirm the current rules before launch. Until then, assume nothing about duration, duplicates, or exclusivity.

A click on a future call button would not be a completed call. A long call would not automatically be a payable call. Those distinctions matter to the operator. They do not change what you owe a contractor you actually hire.

## What the provider owes you {#what-the-provider-owes}

The provider owes you whatever the contract, the license, and the permit require. This site cannot waive a permit, insure the work, or warranty a repair. If a provider’s license, insurance, or review is ever displayed, it will be named as that provider’s and checked against the routing that is actually in use. Nothing of that kind is displayed now. The [about page](/about/) explains the missing identity rather than filling it.

:::faq
Q: Do you negotiate the price with the plumber?
A: No. Negotiating the construction contract is one of the things the licensing board’s marketplace guidance says a referral service should not do. You would hear the provider’s scope and decide whether to hire them. This website will not counter-offer and will not add a site fee to the invoice. If a caller is told otherwise on a live line, that script is outside what this page authorizes.

Q: Why won’t you say what the referral is paid?
A: Because the current terms were not verified, and an invented figure would be worse than silence. Callers do not pay that compensation to this website. It is not a line item in a plumbing quote. Operators need the real rules before launch. Callers need the provider’s price, which this site does not set.

Q: What if the provider is not licensed for the work?
A: Then they should not be presented as the contractor for that work. This referral service does not hold out a license of its own. A production process has to screen the providers it actually connects. That screening is not finished, which is one reason the preview will not dial. If you hire someone, you can check the license with the Contractors State License Board yourself. This page is not that lookup.
:::
