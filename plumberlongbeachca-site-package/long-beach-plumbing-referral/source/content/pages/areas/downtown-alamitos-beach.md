Downtown and Alamitos Beach are grouped here because callers use those names for the same south-west end of Long Beach, not because the City drew one plumbing district. The postal sections discussed on this page are 90802 and 90813. Both codes spill past any single neighborhood. An address, not the ZIP, tells you whether you are in a tower, a bungalow court, or a house on a lettered street.

The City’s Drake Park / Willmore historic district is the housing fact that matters. It combines two earlier districts and sits inside Long Beach’s original 1881 street plan. The City says it holds the highest concentration of early-1900s housing in the city, both single-family houses and apartments, in Victorian, Craftsman, Mission, Prairie, Italian Renaissance, and Spanish Colonial Revival forms. The designated streets include Loma Vista Drive, Park Court, 4th Street, Magnolia Avenue, and an irregular edge back toward Nylic Court and 7th Street. That list is a preservation boundary. It is not a pipe-condition survey, and it does not cover every building in 90802.

Alamitos Beach, closer to the shore, is a different pattern again: apartments, small lots, and houses that may or may not fall inside a historic overlay. Bluff Heights, which the City traces to the Alamitos Beach Townsite, is discussed on the [Belmont Shore and Heights](/areas/belmont-shore-heights/) page because that is where the ordinance’s streets sit. Do not use this downtown page to describe Junipero, Redondo, or Ocean Boulevard’s bluff.

## Who can approve the work {#who-can-approve}

A leak in a stacked building is often a permission problem before it is a plumbing problem. The wet ceiling may belong to a unit whose owner is not home. The shutoff may be in a closet the association controls. The lateral in the garage may serve more than one stack. The person calling should know whether they are the owner, the tenant, or the manager, and who can let a provider into the affected unit and the common area.

This referral service does not contact building management and does not keep a list of downtown associations. If you are a tenant, ask the owner before you authorize a wall to be opened. If you manage the building, say so, and say whether the fixture is in a dwelling or in a common laundry. [Commercial](/services/commercial-plumbing/#commercial-plumbing) calls are a different intake from a residence, even when they share 90802.

## Private lines, shared lines, and the city main {#private-and-shared}

For a Long Beach address, the utility’s lateral split still applies: building to property line on the owner, property line to the main as a shared section the owner maintains, and the main on the city. In a condo or a fourplex the “owner” may be an association or a group of owners. The [sewer guide](/services/sewer-repair/#sewer-lines) walks through that language. It does not identify the cleanout in a particular lobby.

A single-fixture clog stays on the [drain page](/services/drain-cleaning/#drain-cleaning-unclogging). Water that appears with no fixture in use belongs on the [leak page](/services/leak-detection/#leaks-detected-repaired). A heater in a locked closet is the [water heater](/services/water-heaters/#water-heater-repair-or-service) conversation, plus the question of who holds the closet key. Gas odor is not any of those. Leave and call Long Beach Utilities or 911.

Nothing in Willmore’s age proves galvanized pipe, a slab leak, or a failed lateral. Remodels since 1905 have replaced a lot of piping the district survey never recorded. Ask for a look at the fixture or a camera where the symptom is a repeat backup. Do not buy a repipe because the house is in a historic district.

:::faq
Q: Is the building’s drain line private or shared?
A: Inside one unit, a trap and a branch are usually that unit’s plumbing. A stack or a lateral may serve other units, and the person who can open a cleanout may be the association or the owner, not the resident. Long Beach Utilities still distinguishes the building-to-property-line lateral from the shared section and the city main. Which of those you are looking at is a site question. The downtown ZIP does not answer it.

Q: I rent. What should I tell a plumber, and who should call?
A: Describe the symptom and say that you are the tenant. The provider needs the owner or manager if the work goes beyond a simple visit the lease allows, and especially if a wall, a slab, or a shared line would be opened. Have the unit number and the building’s access rules. This website will not call your landlord. If sewage is in the street, call the utility whether you rent or own.

Q: The downstairs drain backed up. Is that the city sewer?
A: It might be a branch, a shared building line, or the lateral, and only sometimes the public main. Street sewage and a surcharging manhole are utility calls at the published water and sewer emergency number. A backup that stays inside, with a dry street, still deserves a look at the building side. Ask for a camera before you accept a story about the main. Neighborhood age does not decide it.
:::
